# Kipling Reference — stories, colour, and how we use them

Kipling is the literary bloodstream of this world. Two strands of his Indian
work are the *closest existing thing to this game's voice*:

- **`Plain Tales from the Hills`** (1888) — forty short Anglo-Indian tales of
  Simla society, office rivalry, careerism, love affairs, and administrative
  callousness. Critics note their "**negative view of British India** —
  bureaucratic infighting and incompetence, a struggle for easy and lucrative
  postings, cruelty in personal relationships." That *is* our tone.
- **`Departmental Ditties`** (1886) — satirical verse whose target is "the
  government office… recast as a staging ground for scandal, nepotism, and
  indolence." (The book was even bound to look like an ICS envelope.) Pure
  tragicomic-bureaucracy fuel.

**A caution on politics.** Kipling gives us matchless *plots, colour, and
characters*, but his imperial romance ("the white man's burden," the
"martial races," the sahib's tragic nobility) is exactly what our ambiguous,
tragicomic frame holds at arm's length. We borrow the world and the wit; we let
the player judge the empire. Where a Kipling story endorses the Raj, our version
usually lets the same events cut both ways.

## Stories → what each gives the game

| Story | The seed | Use |
|-------|----------|-----|
| **Tods' Amendment** *(Plain Tales)* | A small English child, Tods, who plays with the servants and speaks the vernacular, hears at a dinner what the bazaar *really* thinks of a new land-revenue law, and pipes up — the Legal Member amends the Act. | The district's truth comes from below, past the files. **Event: a child amends the enactment.** |
| **At the End of the Passage** *(Life's Handicap)* | Hummil, an overworked engineer in a cholera district, cracked by heat, loneliness and overwork, sees an apparition of himself and dies. The official reports never mention any of it. | The isolation that kills. Deepens **Composure / the Breakdown ending**. **Event: a colleague going to pieces at a lonely out-station.** |
| **Thrown Away** *(Plain Tales)* | "The Boy," raised soft, cannot bear India's rebukes and isolation and shoots himself; his friends write it up as cholera to spare his mother. | Callousness and the merciful lie. **Event: a young officer's suicide and the cover-up.** |
| **The Head of the District** *(Life's Handicap)* | A Bengali gentleman is appointed to head a turbulent Pathan tract that will not be ruled by a non-martial "babu"; a rising follows. | The system's ugly frictions of race and "fitness to rule." **Event: install the unpopular man, or not.** |
| **The Man Who Would Be King** | Two ragged European loafers, Dravot and Carnehan, go beyond the frontier to crown themselves kings; hubris destroys them. | The rogue-adventurer colour. **Event: two loafers pass through with a mad scheme.** |
| **The Bridge-Builders** *(The Day's Work)* | Findlayson's great railway bridge over the Ganges, near completion, is threatened by a generational flood; adrift on the river he dreams the gods of India debating. | Pride in the works vs. the river. **Monsoon event: save the bridge or the villages.** |
| **William the Conqueror** *(The Day's Work)* | A young Englishwoman joins a southern **famine** relief camp; officers work themselves to ruin feeding "the little brown babies" on goat's milk. | Famine-relief texture and the relief-camp romance. Enriches the existing famine. |
| **The Enlightenment of Pagett, M.P.** | A Radical MP, sure the Congress speaks for all India, is shown the real, fractured country by his administrator friend until his certainties wilt in the heat. | The metropolitan who "understands India." **Comic event: chaperone the visiting MP** — cutting, in our version, at *both* his ignorance and the ICS's smugness. |
| **The Story of Uriah** *(Departmental Ditties)* | Jack Barrett is posted to deadly Quetta so a powerful man can have his wife; he dies on schedule. A David-and-Uriah in the Warrant of Precedence. | Simla intrigue at its blackest. **Event: a request to post a junior to a fatal station.** |
| **Miss Youghal's Sais / Strickland** | Strickland the policeman goes undercover *as a native* for months, holding "the extraordinary theory that a Policeman should know as much about the natives as the natives themselves." | Intelligence bought by deception. **Recurring character: Strickland offers you what he's learned.** |
| **Mrs Hauksbee** *(Plain Tales)* | The witty Simla hostess who makes and unmakes reputations and careers over tea — a woman working the male machinery of Empire from the drawing-room. | The soft power of the hills. **Recurring character: cultivate her, or don't.** |
| **Without Benefit of Clergy** | Holden's secret, loving household with Ameera and their child, unknown to the station, ends in cholera and erasure — a happiness "beyond the pale" the Raj cannot allow. | The human cost of the colour line. A future personal-thread beat, handled with restraint and dignity. |
| **The Mark of the Beast** / **Beyond the Pale** | A drunk sahib desecrates a shrine and is cursed; love across "the pale" is punished. | Sacrilege-and-reckoning colour (cf. our procession event); the peril of crossing lines. |
| **Kim** / the **Great Game** | Espionage on the Grand Trunk Road and the frontier. | `MOST SECRET` intelligence flavour; a future frontier subplot. |

## Recurring characters borrowed (with our own names alongside the district's)

- **Mrs Hauksbee** — the Simla intriguer; enters in the hot-weather / hill events.
  Distinct from our local Club empress **Mrs Fortescue-Vane**: Hauksbee is
  *career* power, the Vane is *station* power.
- **Strickland** — the undercover police officer; the "know your district" theme
  with a moral price.
- **Pagett, M.P.** — the visiting politician; a one-off comic set-piece.
- Kept from our own cast: **Ram Autar** (the indispensable clerk, cf. Kipling's
  babus), **Lala Hukum Chand** (the bania/moneylender), **Pandit Girdhari**
  (the Congress pleader — Kipling's bête noire, our sympathetic thorn),
  **Commissioner Blyth-Curram**, **Mukherjee** (the abler deputy).
