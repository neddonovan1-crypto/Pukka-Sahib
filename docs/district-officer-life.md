# Research Brief — The Life of a District Officer in India

Background for design. The player character is a **District Magistrate &
Collector** (in some provinces styled **Deputy Commissioner**). This brief
gathers what that life actually was, so the game's structure, seasons, and
events grow from the real thing rather than a costume drama of it. A "Design
implications" note follows each section; §7 pulls them together.

---

## 1. The office — one man, three hats

The post was created in **1772** by Warren Hastings (revenue collection);
Cornwallis's **1793** reforms split revenue from justice; after the **1857**
Mutiny the Crown re-fused authority at the district into a single commanding
figure. The result — the combination of **fiscal, judicial and executive**
power in one office — made the Collector-Magistrate **the most powerful
official in the district**, governing anywhere from one to several *million*
people with a tiny European staff.

The three hats:

- **Collector** (revenue) — land revenue was the sinew of the Raj. Boundary
  disputes, settlement of fair rents, remission/suspension of revenue in bad
  years, keeping the land records straight.
- **Magistrate** (justice & order) — tried all but the gravest offences (up to
  **two years' hard labour and a ₹1,000 fine**), committed the worst cases to
  the Sessions court, and *was* the district's law and order.
- **Executive** (everything else) — every department in the district — the
  Superintendent of Police, the jail, the Civil Surgeon, the Forest Officer,
  the Executive Engineer — **reported to him**. He was the hub.

> *Design implication:* our three meters **Revenue / Order / Contentment** map
> almost exactly onto the three hats. This is not a coincidence to hide but a
> spine to lean on: an event should usually make the player *feel* which hat
> they're wearing, and the good moves in one hat cost him in another.

## 2. A year in the life — the seasonal rhythm *(the missing spine)*

The district officer's year had a **strong, fixed shape**, and it is the single
most useful thing this research offers the game:

- **Cold weather (≈ Nov–Feb): the touring season.** The officer left
  headquarters and went **into camp** — tents, a line of servants, marching
  village to village across the *mofussil*, holding office in a tent by day and
  night. This is when he saw his district with his own eyes.
- **Hot weather (≈ Mar–Jun): the plains bake.** Work contracts to
  headquarters; those who could — and above all the **memsahibs and children** —
  fled to the **hill stations** (Simla, Mussoorie, Darjeeling, Naini Tal, Ooty),
  sometimes for months. The officer often stayed below, alone, in the heat.
- **The monsoon (≈ Jul–Sep): crisis season.** Flood, failed rains, cholera,
  fever. Roads dissolve; the district turns inward and dangerous.

> *Design implication:* replace the flat 24-fortnight deck with this **calendar
> as the game's backbone**. The deck weights by season — touring events and
> revenue in the cold weather, isolation and Club life and heat in the hot
> weather, disaster in the monsoon. It gives the year narrative shape, makes the
> famine/plague crises land *when they historically would*, and turns "should I
> send my wife to the hills?" into a real recurring decision.

## 3. Two working days — the tent and the cutcherry

**In camp (on tour):** ride into a village with the staff; pitch the camp
office; inspect the **crops, the land records (the *patwari*'s books), the
school, the dispensary, the police station (*thana*), the pound, the jail**;
hear cultivators' grievances on the spot; settle tenancy and rent disputes;
note where revenue must be remitted. Then march to the next village. On return,
write it all up — tour diary, reports, records brought up to date.

**At headquarters (the cutcherry):** the file. Petitions, the day's court work,
revenue paperwork, correspondence up to the Commissioner and down to the
*tahsildars*, the endless **noting** on files. The desk-sim we already have.

> *Design implication:* this is a natural **mode / location mechanic**. Touring
> raises *Contentment* and your grip on the district (you learn things, you're
> seen) but costs *Composure* and lets the HQ paperwork pile up; staying at the
> desk clears files and pleases Simla (*Prestige*) but slowly blinds you to the
> district (*Order/Contentment* drift). A recurring "where will you spend this
> fortnight?" choice.

## 4. The multifarious duties — the job's real texture

Contemporaries described the work as *multifarious*, and the list is almost
comic in its range. A single officer might, in a season, be called on to:

- frame the district **budget** and press the **revenue** collection;
- **try criminal cases** and hear **revenue appeals**;
- run a **famine relief** campaign — grain-for-labour on roads and tanks;
- lay out a **cholera camp** or quarantine a bazaar in an epidemic;
- **hunt down dacoits** (armed bandits), or shoot a man-eating tiger the
  villagers can't;
- manage a **religious festival or fair** without a riot (processions, the
  question of which route past which mosque or temple);
- inspect **schools, dispensaries, jails, ferries, pounds, and roads**;
- entertain the **Commissioner** on inspection and the **Club** on Saturday;
- and, when a crowd turned, give — or refuse — the order to **open fire**.

> *Design implication:* we already dramatise a good slice of this (revenue,
> famine, plague, the tiger, the court, the Congress meeting). The gaps worth
> filling: a **religious festival / procession** event (the classic
> communal-tension test), **dacoits / a crime outbreak**, a **flood**, and a
> **budget** moment. Each is a known historical set-piece.

## 5. The human cost — isolation and the second front

This is the seam the tragicomic tone most wants:

- Young men, often barely into their twenties, posted **thousands of miles from
  home**, frequently the **only European** — or one of a tiny handful — for a
  hundred miles.
- **Loneliness and melancholia** were common enough to be named; the secluded
  *mofussil* station broke people quietly. Drink was the usual anaesthetic.
- The **memsahib's** parallel ordeal: heat, servants, disease, the danger of
  childbirth, and above all the wrenching convention of **sending children
  "home" to England** for schooling — a family split across an ocean as the
  price of the career.
- Club life at an up-country station was "limited in every sense" — a few
  Europeans meeting at tennis and the bar, the same faces, the same feuds.

> *Design implication:* a **personal-life second front** — the *Composure* meter
> deepened into a real strand. A wife who may or may not come out, or stay, or
> go to the hills; children and the letter that asks when they'll be sent home;
> the bottle in the long evening (already in the game). It makes the "pukka
> sahib" mask mean something: the cost of keeping up appearances is paid *here*.

## 6. Relations — the people who actually ran the district

The lone sahib governed **through** others, and the drama lives in those
relationships:

- **Subordinate Indian officials** — the *tahsildar*, the head clerk
  (*sarishtadar* / our Ram Autar), the *patwari* who keeps the land records —
  who did the real work and knew what the sahib never would. Often abler than
  him (our Mukherjee).
- **The landholders and moneylenders** — *zamindars* and the *bania*/Lala who
  held the district's debts and could make revenue flow or freeze.
- **The notables and the new politics** — the Congress pleader, the mill-owner,
  the men organising the 1920s non-cooperation stirrings.
- **The peasantry (*ryots*)** — the source of the revenue and the ones a famine
  or a lathi-charge actually fell on.

> *Design implication:* build the recurring cast into **relationship arcs** with
> memory — trust with Ram Autar unlocks (already prototyped), debt to the Lala
> compounds, the Pandit escalates or is co-opted. A light faction/standing model
> behind the meters.

---

## 7. Design implications, gathered

1. **Adopt the seasonal calendar** (cold-weather tour → hot-weather station →
   monsoon crisis) as the game's temporal spine; weight the deck by season.
2. **Add the tour ⇄ cutcherry mode choice** — where you spend a fortnight, with
   real trade-offs.
3. **Keep the three-hats framing** (Revenue/Order/Contentment) explicit.
4. **Open the personal-life second front** (wife, children, the hills, the
   bottle) to give *Composure* weight and the mask its meaning.
5. **Fill the historical set-piece gaps**: a festival/procession, dacoits, a
   flood, a budget.
6. **Turn the cast into relationship arcs** with memory and consequence.
