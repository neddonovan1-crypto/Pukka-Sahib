# Pukka Sahib — Period Reference: the Language & Imagery of the ICS

A working reference for writing events, naming things, and dressing the
interface. The aim is **texture, not a history lesson**: enough authentic
vocabulary and imagery that the world feels lived-in and the jokes have real
furniture to sit on. Terms are grouped by how we'll use them.

> A note on accuracy. Where a term or date is well-established it is stated
> plainly; where usage is looser or period-dependent it is flagged. The game is
> set in the **1920s**, so a few WWII-era refinements (see *Secrecy markings*)
> are noted as such. Sources are listed at the foot.

---

## 1. Secrecy & document markings — *the stamps*

This is the imagery you remembered, and it's real. British official secrecy ran
a graded ladder of markings, rubber-stamped and sometimes hand-underlined on the
top-right of a file cover or the head of a minute:

- **Most Secret** — the apex tier. Confirmed in the India Office Records, whose
  most sensitive files were marked *Most Secret* and *Very Secret*. *Historical
  note:* "Most Secret" was the British top grade until c.1941–43, when it was
  renamed **Top Secret** to match the Americans for Allied interoperability. In
  the **1920s** setting, *Most Secret / Very Secret* is the authentic top mark —
  we are period-correct to use it and should **not** use "Top Secret."
- **Secret**
- **Confidential** — the everyday mark for anything politically awkward.
- **For Official Use Only** / **Not for Publication** — routine restriction.
- **Demi-Official (D.O.)** — see §4; not a secrecy grade but a register.

Real institutional flavour to borrow:

- The **Political & Secret Department** of the India Office — a genuine body that
  handled intelligence, princely-state affairs, and the touchy files. (Our
  masthead's "Political Department" can become **Political & Secret Department**.)
- **Cypher / cipher telegram** — sensitive wires were enciphered; a "cypher
  telegram from the Residency" is period-perfect.
- The **Official Secrets Act, 1923** — the legal club behind all of the above.

**In-game use:** these are our rubber-stamp event tags. A famine relief cover-up
stamped **CONFIDENTIAL**; a princely-state intrigue **MOST SECRET**; a scolding
from the Commissioner as a **D.O.** The stamp itself tells a joke about how
seriously the machine takes each thing.

## 2. The Service & its officers — *what they called themselves*

- **The Covenanted Civil Service** → **the Indian Civil Service (ICS)** — the
  elite administrative cadre. Perhaps a thousand men governing ~300 million.
- **"The heaven-born"** — what the *other* services (Police, Forests, Customs,
  the "uncovenanted" men) called ICS officers, with heavy irony. ICS men modestly
  called themselves **"civilians."**
- **"The steel frame"** — the ICS's own grand self-image, from Lloyd George's
  1922 Commons speech: the Service was "the steel frame of the whole structure."
  A district officer was a trestle of that frame. Ripe for tragicomic deflation.
- **"Competition-wallah"** — a man who entered by the competitive examination
  (introduced 1855–56), as opposed to the older **Haileybury** men appointed by
  patronage. Coined, sneeringly, by the patronage generation; the title of an
  1864 book by G. O. Trevelyan. A perfect bit of intra-Service snobbery.
- **"-wallah"** suffix — "man/fellow/agent of": *competition-wallah*,
  *punkah-wallah*, *box-wallah* (a merchant — mild contempt from officials),
  *Congress-wallah*.

**Ranks & posts** (the district ladder):

- **District Magistrate & Collector** — the player. *Magistrate* = criminal
  justice & order; *Collector* = land revenue. One overworked man, both hats.
  Also styled **Deputy Commissioner (D.C.)** in some provinces.
- **Commissioner** — supervises several districts (a Division). The player's boss.
- **Sub-Divisional Officer (SDO)**, **Sub-Deputy Collector** — subordinate
  officers, often Indian; frequently more competent than the sahib.
- **Tahsildar** — Indian revenue officer in charge of a *tahsil*.
- **Resident / Political Agent** — the Crown's man at a princely court.
- **The Secretariat** — the provincial/central government offices; **Simla**, the
  summer capital in the hills, is shorthand for remote higher authority.

## 3. The vocabulary of the district — *Hobson-Jobson country*

The great source is **Hobson-Jobson** (Yule & Burnell, 1886), "A Glossary of
Colloquial Anglo-Indian Words and Phrases" — the coloniser's own slang
dictionary. Terms an officer used daily:

**Places & land**
- **mofussil** — the rural/up-country districts, as opposed to the Presidency
  capital (the **sudder/sadar**). "Out in the mofussil" = the sticks.
- **cutcherry (kutcherry)** — the district office and courthouse; where petitions
  are heard and files pile up. Our whole game happens in and around the cutcherry.
- **zila** — a district. **pargana / tahsil** — subdivisions.
- **dâk bungalow** — a government rest-house on the road (from **dâk**, the
  post/relay system). **civil lines** — the European quarter. **cantonment** —
  the military quarter. **the Club** — the social citadel (see §5).

**People**
- **ryot** — a peasant cultivator (the revenue ultimately comes from him).
- **zamindar** — a landholder / revenue intermediary. **lambardar** — village
  revenue headman. **Lala** — an honorific for a Hindu trader/moneylender (our
  Lala Hukum Chand). **bania** — merchant/moneylender caste.
- **chaprassi** — a uniformed office messenger/peon wearing a brass badge
  (**chaprass**); the human email of the Raj. **khansama** — house steward.
  **punkah-wallah** — pulls the ceiling fan. **syce** — groom. **mali** —
  gardener. **munshi** — clerk/language teacher/secretary.
- **sahib** / **memsahib** — master / European lady. **burra sahib** — the big
  boss; **chota sahib** — the junior. **pukka** — proper, first-class, genuine;
  **kutcha (cutcha)** — makeshift, unfinished, improper. (The title lives here.)

**The daily round**
- **chota hazri** — "little breakfast," early-morning tea. **tiffin** — lunch.
- **chit** — a note/memo/IOU. **dastur** — custom; also the customary
  commission/bribe that oils everything. **bakshish / baksheesh** — a tip or
  gratuity. **dacoit** — armed robber/bandit (a **dacoity** = the crime).
- **shikar** — hunting; **shikari** — a hunter/tracker (cf. the Tiger event).
- **lakh** (100,000) and **crore** (10,000,000) — the units revenue is counted in.
- **jheel** — a lake/marsh. **nullah** — a ravine/watercourse. **maidan** — an
  open ground/parade square. **bandobast** — arrangements, "the fixing-up of
  things" (a very officer word). **gymkhana** — the sports/social club grounds.

## 4. The paper world — *how the machine actually ran*

The ICS ran on files, and the file had a culture. This is the richest seam for a
management sim, because the *bureaucratic form* of a decision is itself the joke.

- **Noting** — officials wrote sequential **notes** on the left-hand "note sheet"
  of a file, working a matter up the hierarchy; the final **order** is passed at
  the top. A file is a slow-motion argument conducted in the margins.
- **P.U.C.** — "Paper Under Consideration," the document a file hangs on.
- **"Put up"** — to submit a file for orders ("please put up the famine file").
- **docket / docketing** — the summary/endorsement on a file's cover or dorse.
- **flag / flagging** — tabs (Flag A, Flag B) marking referenced papers.
- **Demi-Official (D.O.) letter** — a semi-private letter, officer to officer,
  first-person and "My dear Blyth-Curram," used to say the things a formal
  minute can't. The Raj's back-channel. A **D.O. from the Commissioner** is how
  you get quietly warned or quietly threatened.
- Files stamped **Ordinary / Urgent / Immediate** for priority.
- **dâk** — the post; the **dâk runner** carried it between stations. A telegram
  reads in clipped caps with **STOP** for full stops (already in our game).
- **Blue Books** — official published reports/returns. **the Gazette** — the
  official journal where appointments, notifications and orders were published
  ("gazetted" = officially appointed).
- **red tape** — literally the pinkish-red cotton tape that bound legal and
  official bundles; the phrase for bureaucratic obstruction comes straight from
  this. Our accent colour *is* the red tape.

## 5. Society, honours & precedence — *the game above the game*

- **The Club** — the European social fortress; membership, the bar, billiards,
  the cold-weather ball, and rigid unwritten law. Exclusion from it is a real
  sanction. (Orwell's *Burmese Days* turns on Club politics.)
- **The Warrant of Precedence** — an actual ranked table fixing who outranks whom
  at every official and social occasion, **including wives**. Disputes over it
  were bitter and absurd — exactly our "Question of Precedence" event.
- **Honours** (what ambition is *for*):
  - **The Most Exalted Order of the Star of India** (1861) — senior. Grades:
    **GCSI / KCSI / CSI**. Motto: *"Heaven's Light Our Guide."*
  - **The Most Eminent Order of the Indian Empire** (1878/1887) — junior, the
    workhorse honour for officials. Grades: **GCIE / KCIE / CIE**. Motto:
    *"Imperatricis Auspiciis"* ("Under the auspices of the Empress").
  - A **CIE** is the realistic ambition of a good district man; a **KCIE**
    (a knighthood — "Sir") the dream. Our "K.C.I.E." ending is the brass ring.
- The **New Year** and **Birthday Honours** lists — when careers were made or,
  by omission, quietly ended.

## 6. Material & visual imagery — *for the interface & art*

Concrete objects to render, name-drop, or stamp:

- **Rubber stamps & sealing wax** — the classification marks; a **seal** roundel
  (our I.C.S. crest); wax seals in oxblood.
- **The dispatch box** — the officer's red/black document box; the tin **uniform
  case** and **deed boxes** in the record room.
- **The file & the note sheet** — treasury-tag string, red tape, dockets, "flags."
- **Dress** — the **solar topi / topee** (pith helmet); white drill; the
  **Warrant**-fixed order of a mess dinner; the **Collector's** dusty touring kit.
- **The touring camp** — the D.O. "on tour" in the *mofussil*, the tent, the
  camp table, the line of petitioners.
- **Print** — the **Gazette**, the **Blue Book**, the district **Gazetteer**
  (a fat reference volume on every district), Survey of India maps.
- **The Residency / Government House** — the grand architecture of authority.
- **The heat** — the punkah, the hot-weather exodus to the hills (**Simla**), the
  monsoon, cholera in the bazaar — the environmental antagonist.

## 7. How we'll use it

- **Event tags become real markings.** Swap generic tags for stamped grades and
  registers: `MOST SECRET`, `CONFIDENTIAL`, `D.O.`, `TELEGRAM`, `CYPHER`,
  `GAZETTE`, `FROM THE MOFUSSIL`. The stamp editorialises the event.
- **The file as the frame.** Present events as **notes on a file** working up to
  the player's **order** — the player writes the final minute. Ties the UI to §4.
- **Vocabulary, lightly.** Salt the prose with *cutcherry, dastur, bandobast,
  pukka, chit, dâk* — glossed by context, never a wall of italics. A first-use
  tooltip glossary keeps it painless.
- **Honours as the ambition meter.** Frame Prestige explicitly around the
  **CIE → KCIE** ladder and the Honours List; near-misses sting.
- **Masthead upgrade.** "Political & Secret Department," an ICS seal, and a
  *Most Secret* corner-stamp on the right cover — all now sourced.
